Grocery list and store trip

shopping · overlap-shopping-01

The shared grocery list is the record of household purchases for the next planned trip. Entries distinguish shared supplies from requests that a resident plans to buy separately. Each shared item has a quantity and any essential details, such as an unscented product or a package that fits the available shelf. Residents check the shelf and the open packages before adding another entry. This helps the shopper understand whether a low count means the item is needed immediately or merely worth watching. Items reserved for a specific recipe also include the date they will be used.

If the store is out of a shared grocery item, use the substitute only when the list entry names an approved alternative. If there is no approved alternative, leave the original item unchecked and add the note Out of stock. Do not mark the item purchased when no acceptable product was obtained. The next shopper can retry the original entry without reconstructing the request from chat. If the approved substitute is purchased, record its actual name in the list entry before closing it. This allows residents to find the substitute when they check the pantry later.

Grocery list and store trip

shopping · overlap-shopping-02

If the store is out of a shared grocery item, use the substitute only when the list entry names an approved alternative. If there is no approved alternative, leave the original item unchecked and add the note Out of stock. Do not mark the item purchased when no acceptable product was obtained. The next shopper can retry the original entry without reconstructing the request from chat. If the approved substitute is purchased, record its actual name in the list entry before closing it. This allows residents to find the substitute when they check the pantry later.

After returning from a grocery trip, the shopper matches the contents of each bag to the shared list before updating the purchase status. Purchased supplies are marked complete on their original entries, and items that were skipped remain visible for the next trip. The list includes a short field for the actual quantity when a store sells a different package size. A shopper who bought less than requested records the amount still needed. The household reviews these outstanding items before planning another trip. Keeping the list current reduces duplicate purchases and makes it easier to share errands.
