# Home maintenance coordination

## Bathroom tap maintenance requests

Residents use the maintenance board to report problems with household fixtures. A useful report describes the fixture, the room, the symptom, and when it was noticed. A clear description makes it easier for the coordinator to recognize whether several residents are reporting the same problem. Photos can be useful if the issue is visible and the resident can take them without handling the fixture. If a photo cannot be taken easily, a written description is sufficient to start a report. The household coordinator checks the board to decide what follow-up is needed and records who will handle it.

For a slow drip from the bathroom tap, open a Plumbing card on the Maintenance board. Write the room, the time you first noticed the drip, and whether it continues with the handle fully closed. Attach a photo if you can take one safely. Check for an existing card for that fixture before opening a new one, and add your observation to the existing card if it is already listed. Notify the household maintenance coordinator through that card. This is the household reporting workflow for a slow tap drip and leaves repair decisions with the coordinator.

The coordinator reviews tap reports together with earlier maintenance notes for the same fixture. If a visit is planned, the coordinator records the appointment status and any access arrangements on the card. Residents add new observations to that card when the symptom changes, which keeps the account of the problem in one place. A card remains open while an inspection or repair is still pending. After the work is done, record the completion date and a short description of the result. This record helps the household distinguish a recurring issue from a new observation during future checks.

## Scheduled appliance upkeep

The appliance upkeep list contains routine checks for shared equipment such as the vacuum and the laundry machines. Each appliance has its own card so residents can see which equipment has been checked and what still needs attention. When adding a planned task, the coordinator records the appliance name, the item to inspect, and the target week. Residents consult the manufacturer's instructions for equipment-specific steps. The household task board is a scheduling and recordkeeping tool. It can track where the instructions are stored and identify who agreed to perform the next check.

Spare appliance consumables are stored near the shared cleaning shelf and labeled with the equipment they fit. Residents check the part description before taking a replacement because different models can use similar-looking parts. The scheduled upkeep card can list an item that needs replenishing, while the shopping list is used to request the purchase. When a consumable is installed, the resident records the change date on the appliance card. Any unused pieces return to their labeled location. This gives the next reviewer a clear account of what was replaced and what remains in reserve.

A service visit for a shared appliance has a separate appointment record linked to the appliance card. The resident coordinating the visit confirms which appliance the technician will inspect and prepares access to its normal location. Before the visit, place relevant instructions and earlier service notes where the coordinator can retrieve them. Afterward, update the appointment record with the actual work performed and any follow-up recommended by the technician. The service record can be reviewed during the next scheduled upkeep check. It does not close unrelated maintenance requests for bathroom fixtures or other equipment.
