# Shopping and shared purchases

## Grocery list and store trip

The shared grocery list is the record of household purchases for the next planned trip. Entries distinguish shared supplies from requests that a resident plans to buy separately. Each shared item has a quantity and any essential details, such as an unscented product or a package that fits the available shelf. Residents check the shelf and the open packages before adding another entry. This helps the shopper understand whether a low count means the item is needed immediately or merely worth watching. Items reserved for a specific recipe also include the date they will be used.

If the store is out of a shared grocery item, use the substitute only when the list entry names an approved alternative. If there is no approved alternative, leave the original item unchecked and add the note Out of stock. Do not mark the item purchased when no acceptable product was obtained. The next shopper can retry the original entry without reconstructing the request from chat. If the approved substitute is purchased, record its actual name in the list entry before closing it. This allows residents to find the substitute when they check the pantry later.

After returning from a grocery trip, the shopper matches the contents of each bag to the shared list before updating the purchase status. Purchased supplies are marked complete on their original entries, and items that were skipped remain visible for the next trip. The list includes a short field for the actual quantity when a store sells a different package size. A shopper who bought less than requested records the amount still needed. The household reviews these outstanding items before planning another trip. Keeping the list current reduces duplicate purchases and makes it easier to share errands.

## Receipts and reimbursement

Shared receipts are entered in the purchase log with the shop name, date, and total for the household portion. A receipt that includes personal purchases should have the household items identified in the log entry so the reimbursement total can be checked without guessing. The resident who paid keeps the paper receipt until the log is reconciled. A clear photo can be attached if the printed receipt is likely to fade. The purchase log records amounts already spent and the person who paid. The grocery list remains the place to record items that are still needed.

The reimbursement review happens after receipts for the period have been entered. Residents check the shared amounts and agree on the reimbursements that remain open. Each reimbursement record identifies the matching purchase log entries, the resident who paid, and the amount being repaid. Mark a repayment complete after the transfer has actually occurred. If an amount is disputed, keep it open and add a short description of the discrepancy for discussion. The review helps residents resolve costs without losing the original receipt details. It has no effect on the completion status of unpurchased grocery entries.

A return to a shop needs its own record when the original transaction included a shared purchase. The resident returning the item links the refund to the original receipt and records the actual amount received. If the store offers store credit, the log records that form of refund and the person holding it. The household agrees how shared credit will be used on a later trip. Before replacing a returned item, check whether it is still on the grocery list or whether the household has already obtained a replacement. This check avoids buying the same supply twice.
